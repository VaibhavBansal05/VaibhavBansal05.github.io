Autonomous Robot Exploration and Mapping

A differential-drive robot maps an unknown maze, selects unexplored frontiers, and navigates to them without a preloaded map. The interesting work was making sensing, odometry, SLAM, and navigation agree well enough to complete a run.

How the system works

01 / SENSE

Scan the maze

A 360° GPU LiDAR supplies obstacle ranges while the differential-drive model publishes odometry.

02 / MAP

Update SLAM

SLAM Toolbox combines scans and odometry into an occupancy map of known, free, and unknown space.

03 / CHOOSE

Find frontiers

explore_lite chooses boundaries between mapped free space and unexplored cells.

04 / MOVE

Navigate and repeat

Nav2 plans to the selected frontier; new scans reveal more space and the cycle continues.

The stack integrates existing ROS 2 packages. My contribution was constructing and debugging the robot, simulation, sensing and navigation configuration, and their end-to-end operation.

03

Three engineering decisions that mattered

Fixing distorted maps

A configured 0.40 m wheel separation disagreed with the robot's 0.36 m joint geometry. Turns produced incorrect odometry, so successive scans were placed at the wrong angles. Matching the configuration to the geometry resolved the map shearing.

Replacing the sensor pipeline

The RGB-D-to-laserscan route was narrow in view and expensive on the available computer. Moving to GPU LiDAR removed the conversion bottleneck and restored the reported control-loop rate from 1.3 Hz to 20 Hz.

Making exploration meaningful

A wide scan could reveal the initial small maze immediately, leaving no frontier to pursue. Sight-blocking corridors kept new space hidden until the robot moved into it.

Keeping navigation feasible

Costmap inflation made narrow corridors appear blocked. I tuned the robot radius, inflation and angular speed for the maze, and removed a competing odometry transform publisher.

04

Evidence and scope

This is a simulated exploration demonstration and an integration and debugging case study. The video shows one run; it does not establish reliability across arbitrary maps or physical robots. The public repository contains the configuration and launch files, plus an earlier development clip.
