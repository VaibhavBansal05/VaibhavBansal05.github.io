About

I'm an undergraduate researcher working at the intersection of robotics, computer vision, and machine learning. Most of my work is about closing the gap between a learned model and a robot that actually has to fly: state estimation without GPS, detection pipelines that hold their frame rate on an embedded board, and planners that respect hard temporal constraints.

At AeroMIT I lead the autonomous drone research subsystem, where I architect real-time RGBD and 360° LiDAR fusion pipelines for state estimation and environment surveying in GPS-denied zones, and build ROS2/MAVLink autonomy stacks validated in Gazebo before they touch hardware.

At the HIRO Lab, IISc Bangalore, I worked on a JAX-based Model-Based Diffusion planner that enforces differentiable Signal Temporal Logic constraints for non-holonomic robots — scoring thousands of candidate trajectories in parallel on GPU and pairing the offline planner with an online receding-horizon controller for reactive obstacle avoidance.

I care about systems that are measurable: cross-validation schemes that don't leak, planners with provable constraint satisfaction, and pipelines whose latency budget you can actually write down. That instinct produced my first-author paper at IC3-2026, where embedding kinematic constraints into a gradient-boosting solver beat every unconstrained baseline on cross-vehicle generalization.

Sounding Rocket Flight-Phase Classification

code ↗

Physics-informed ML · Basis of the IC3-2026 paper · 7 EuRoC flights

  • Classifies flight phase — boost, coast, apogee, descent, landed — from GPS telemetry alone, with no barometric pressure sensor available.
  • PhaseNet: multi-scale temporal convolutional encoders with cross-scale attention fusion and a physics-informed constraint loss that rules out kinematically impossible predictions.
  • PI-XGB: custom-objective XGBoost embedding penalty matrices directly into the gradient and Hessian to enforce energy conservation and valid state transitions.
  • Best mean macro-F1 of 0.838 (±0.112) under Leave-One-Rocket-Out cross-validation, ahead of stock XGBoost (0.830) and Random Forest (0.823); macro-F1 chosen so the majority descent class cannot mask failures on safety-critical boost and apogee.
  • 13 engineered kinematic and statistical features; SHAP and LIME used for model explainability.

Research & Publications

Physics-Informed Gradient Boosting and Explainable AI for Real-Time Sounding Rocket Phase Classification Accepted · Presented

Vaibhav Krishna Bansal, Rudra Rajpurohit, Vedant Totla, Gogulamudi Pradeep Reddy, U. Shivani Sri Varshini

IC3-2026 — 18th International Conference on Contemporary Computing · JIIT Noida & University of Florida · Aug 2026 · in press, IEEE Xplore

First-author paper introducing PI-XGB, which embeds kinematic penalty matrices directly into XGBoost's gradient and Hessian to suppress physically impossible flight-state transitions. Classifies five flight phases from GPS telemetry alone — for vehicles carrying no barometric pressure sensor, removing the conventional apogee signal used to trigger parachute deployment. Mean macro-F1 of 0.838 ± 0.112 under strict Leave-One-Rocket-Out cross-validation across seven EuRoC flights, ahead of standard XGBoost (0.830), Random Forest (0.823), Bi-LSTM (0.729) and SVM (0.713). SHAP and LIME confirm the models learn aerodynamic decision boundaries rather than dataset artefacts.

Technically co-sponsored by the IEEE Computer Society · ~35% acceptance rate from ~915 submissions
